An overview

Homotopy, with an algebraic interval.

Motivic homotopy theory brings the methods of homotopy theory to algebraic geometry, using the affine line A¹ in place of the ordinary interval. This thesis surveys the constructions of the unstable and stable motivic categories, drawing connections between their different models.

The second thread is synthetic spectra: an approach built from spectra and spherical sheaves, with a natural bigrading and connections to motivic homotopy. The thesis develops the foundations for these connections and discusses applications to stable homotopy theory.

Three threads through the thesis

A thematic reading guide, not a chapter index.

Homotopical foundations

Simplicial sets, model categories, and sheaves: the language needed to transport homotopical ideas into geometry.

The motivic setting

A¹-localization, P¹-stabilization, and cellularity. Different constructions of the motivic categories, and how they fit together.

The synthetic perspective

Spherical sheaves and synthetic spectra. Bigradings, the role of τ, and connections to motivic and classical stable homotopy.
